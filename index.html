Whistling Coach

Learn to whistle in tune — for iPhone and iPad.

Whistling Coach exists for one particular person: someone who wants to whistle a tune and is a long way off the notes. Not slightly flat. Off.

The problem it solves

If you are badly off pitch, the hard part is not effort. It is that you cannot hear your own error. That is not a failure of attention — matching a pitch requires a comparison your ear is not yet making, and no amount of trying harder supplies it.

Which is why being told "wrong, try again" teaches almost nothing. It arrives after the attempt is over, and it says nothing about which way to move or how far. What is missing is a way to see what your ear cannot yet tell you — continuously, while the note is still sounding, so that correcting it becomes something you can steer rather than guess at.

That is the whole app. It draws the pitch you are making, live, against the note you are aiming at. You watch the gap and close it.

How a practice session goes

Whistling Coach plays a note. You whistle it. Nothing moves on until you have actually held it — not brushed past it on the way somewhere else, held it, for long enough that it counts as deliberate. Sliding across the right pitch and landing on it are different skills, and only one of them is worth rewarding.

Then it waits for you to stop before it plays anything else, so the app is never talking over you.

A brief break — a breath, a wobble — pauses the timer rather than resetting it. Nothing kills the will to continue faster than a progress bar dropping to zero at 95%.

Built for people who are a long way off

Most tuners assume you are nearly right and show you the last few cents. Whistling Coach assumes nothing.

The target is a band, and its thickness is the tolerance — so an easier setting is visibly a wider target rather than a smaller number you have to take on trust. Behind your pitch runs a two-second trail, which is where the useful information actually lives: it shows the habits a single reading cannot, like starting sharp and sagging, or creeping up from underneath every single time.

And when a note is genuinely beyond you, the app widens the band rather than letting you fail at it indefinitely. Progress beats purity.

Why whistling, specifically

A whistle is almost a single pure tone. A voice is a stack of tones — the note, plus energy at two, three, four times its frequency, which is what gives a voice its character.

That difference is what makes this app possible. With one clean tone, pitch can be measured to within a few hundredths of a semitone, instantly, in a room that is not quiet. With a voice, the app would first have to work out which rung of the ladder is the real note — the classic way pitch trackers end up an octave out.

So the app measures the ladder directly. One rung: a whistle, and it follows you. Several rungs: a voice, a hum, or a radio in the next room, and it deliberately stays put rather than showing you a confident number that happens to be wrong.

Fitted to your range, not someone else's

Whistling ranges are small and they differ. A song written for a piano is not automatically a song you can whistle.

The app places every song where people can actually whistle it, and if that is still wrong for you, one tap moves the whole tune a semitone at a time. The app remembers, so you do it once. It also learns which octave you tend to land in and stops arguing with you about it.

There is no calibration ritual, no range test to get wrong before you are allowed to begin. An earlier version had one, and it was worse: it asked the person who cannot yet control their pitch to demonstrate the two notes everything would then be scaled to.

Three ways to practise

  • Note by note — hear a note, hold it until it is right
  • Whole tune — hear the first note, then perform the rest yourself while the app follows you
  • Tune and rhythm — the same, with each note held about as long as it is written

And one way to make it a game

Party mode puts several people on the same song, each on their own phone, taking turns while everyone watches the current player's pitch live on their own screen. Points for landing the note, then for accuracy, then for speed — in that order, deliberately, because a scoring rule that mostly rewarded reflexes would teach the wrong thing.

It works over Wi-Fi, or Bluetooth when there is no network at all. No server, no accounts, nothing to set up.

Nothing is recorded

Your whistling is analysed on the device, moment by moment, and then it is gone. Nothing is saved and nothing is sent anywhere. There are no accounts, no analytics and no adverts. Even in Party mode only names and scores travel between phones — never audio.
