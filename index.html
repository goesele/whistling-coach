Whistly

Learn to whistle in tune.

Whistly plays a note. You whistle it. Nothing moves on until you have actually held it — not brushed past it on the way somewhere else, held it.

The pitch you are making is drawn live against the note you are aiming at, so you can see the gap closing instead of guessing.

Built for people who are a long way off

Most tuners assume you are nearly right and show you the last few cents. Whistly assumes nothing. The target is a band whose thickness is the tolerance, so an easier setting is visibly a wider target. A two-second trail follows your pitch, which shows the habits a single number cannot: that you start sharp and sag, or approach from below every time.

It knows what a whistle is

A whistle is almost a single pure tone. A voice is a stack of tones. Whistly measures that difference directly, so it tracks whistling instantly and precisely — and quietly refuses to track humming, singing, or a radio in the next room, rather than showing you a confident number that happens to be wrong.

Fitted to your range

Every song is placed where people can actually whistle it, and if that is still wrong for you, one tap moves the whole tune a semitone at a time. The app remembers. There is no setup and no calibration ritual to fail before you start.

Nothing is recorded

Your whistling is analysed on the device, moment by moment, and then it is gone. Nothing is saved and nothing is sent anywhere. There are no accounts, no analytics and no adverts. In Party mode, where several people play together over Wi-Fi or Bluetooth, only names and scores travel between phones — never audio.
